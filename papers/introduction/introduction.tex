\textbf{\href{https://meskanen.com/abstract/informational-derivation-of-reality.pdf}{Paper~I}} establishes the four observer axioms and derives that
any universe containing an observer must be static, timeless, finite, and substrate-independent. 

\textbf{\href{https://meskanen.com/abstract/black-hole-singularities.pdf}{Paper~II}} applies the framework to black hole singularities and
draws the first structural conclusion. The zero-entropy extremum maps to the black hole singularity,
while the opposing extremum corresponds to the state of maximum entropy.
Between these two bounds, the entropic trajectory follows the time-reversed evolution of a typical emergent, relaxing thermodynamic
system progressing from zero entropy toward full equilibrium.

\textbf{\href{https://meskanen.com/abstract/emergence-of-spacetime.pdf}{Paper~III}} inverts the black hole picture and applies it to
cosmology: expansion from a zero-entropy initial state is the geometric reading of entropy increase, and the two processes are the same
thermodynamic event viewed from opposite ends.

\textbf{\href{https://meskanen.com/abstract/wavefunction-as-compression.pdf}{Paper~IV}} derives the Spectral Complexity measure $C_s$ and
establishes its role as the physical prior over wavefunctions:
$P(\psi) \propto 2^{-C_s(\psi)}$. This is the link between the
information-theoretic framework and quantum mechanics. The wave-like
character of matter is a consequence of spectral compression, not an
additional postulate.

\textbf{\href{https://meskanen.com/abstract/spectral-compression-gravity.pdf}{Paper~V}} studies 
whether spectral complexity, once the geometry is promoted to a variable, contains general relativity.
