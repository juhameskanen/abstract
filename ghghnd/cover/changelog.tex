\section*{Changelog}

This page tracks significant changes across the various releases.

\begin{description}


 \item[\textbf{1988}] - Pain
 Preface written, with the "tooth pain" incident and the question of whether pain could ever be implemented as software.


\item[\textbf{1989}] - Abstract Nature of Everything
  The two first axioms based on observable assumptions about the structure of DNA and the conclusion that human is an axiomatic system, 
  leading to the conclusion that the that the universe must be abstract, static and timeless, emerging from random noise. 


\item[\textbf{1989}] - Wooden Computer and The Mystery
  If a vast network of components in a wooden turing machine create a suffering virtual human, what does a single moving wooden component create?

  Is this \textbf{real} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{virtual} action beyond known physics?


\item[\textbf{1991}] - Human as an Axiomatic System

  The first version of the paper with allfour axioms and the derivation of the conclusion that the universe is static, timeless and random noise.


\item[\textbf{1992}] - Pain Hypothesis

  Conclusion about simulated humans must feel pain, or there would be a mathematical contradiction. 
  

\item[\textbf{1993}] - The Method

  The Method chapter, articulating the key idea that even if we do not know the true nature of reality e.g. time or a black hole singularity, we can simulate them. Whatever they are, their functional equivalents must exist inside the computer running the simulation. Therefore, we can study the computer, whose internal structure we perfectly understand.
are must emerge from the computer running the simulation. Therefore, we draw conclusion by studying the computer, whose internal structure we perfectly understand.


\item[\textbf{1994}] - The Two Time Thought Experiments

  First draft chapter explaining the two time thought experiments: time can be gradually optimized out by replacing simulation code with static data, and a simulated human must still experience pain, otherwise there would be a code/data threshold where pain vanishes. Consciousness must emerge from static data.
  In a multithreaded, preemptive computer system, consciousness inevitably emerges from pure white noise, or there would be a maximum thread count threshold where consciousness vanishes. 
  The fundamental nature of the universe is static, timeless, random \textbf{noise}.

  
  \textit{How could gravity possibly work in static random noise?}


\item[\textbf{1995}] - Black Hole Thought Experiments

  First gravity thought experiment monitoring a black hole simulation of collapsing spacetime and the entropy of its memory image. 
  Discovery: entropy decreases as spacetime collapses. Although the simulation crashes before the singularity forms, we can extrapolate the entropy trend: a singularity is a zero-entropy state.

  Discovery: any attempt to interpret zero-entropy information as geometric space always maps to a single object, a geometric point.

  First Lemma: a singularity is a zero-entropy state, and therefore its geometric spacetime equivalent must be a geometric point.


\item[\textbf{1996}] - Time-Reversed Black Hole Thought Experiment

  Discovery that all geometric interpretations of increasing entropy yield an expanding geometric object.

  Hypothesis: an expanding universe and increasing entropy are two sides of the same coin (information). The universe is expanding because it is increasing in entropy.

  Inflationary expansion of the early universe might be explained by a random system rushing from zero entropy toward thermodynamic equilibrium.



\item[\textbf{1996}] - Emergent Geometric Microstructures

Simulations mapping increasing entropy to geometric space while filtering microstructures reveal a log-normal distribution curve. 
The probability of emergent structure appears to follow a log-normal shape, giving rise to elementary particles.


\item[\textbf{2005}] - Box of Coins Experiment

  Discovery via the "coins in a box" thought experiment: the more ways coins can be arranged to describe a structure, the higher the probability of that structure emerging.

  How do we maximize the probability of emergence? By using some of the bits to describe a compression algorithm!

  Conclusion: A random emergent observer will most likely find themselves residing within a compressed structure.


\item[\textbf{2006}] - Laws of Physics as Compression

  Predictable, smoothly evolving data compresses far better than chaotically jumping data: enter mass and inertia!

  \( \text{Predictability} \rightarrow \text{Compression} \rightarrow \text{Probability} \)

  We most likely find ourselves in a smoothly evolving, predictable universe because predictable information compresses best. That is precisely what the laws of physics represent: predictability.


\item[\textbf{2009}] - Wavefunction as spectral compression

  Discovery that the microcosm waves everywhere, we are observing compressed structure. 
  Animation analogy: the quantum mechanical complex-valued wavefunction is the universe's compression signature; the Born rule acts as its dithering system.

  Thought experiment: render all possible videos of yourself in all possible formats, both compressed and uncompressed. Pick one at random. What do you observe? Most likely a smoothly evolving dithered, compressed video, because bits can be arranged to render you in vastly more ways through compression and dithering.



\item[\textbf{2014}] - Good Heavens Gods? Hell No Dogs!

First draft of the manuscript, with provocative title.


\item[\textbf{2018}] - Why Geometry? Why Not High-Dimensional Spectral?

  Chapter about why we perceive life in a geometric $3+1$ spacetime instead of a high-dimensional complex-valued spectral representation in Hilbert space?


\item[\textbf{2019}] -  The Hard Problem of Consciousness

  Observer equivalence class, and the nature, source and purpose of qualia. 

  Causal vs. Representation Relationship: an $n$-bit information state defines a configuration space of $2^n$ potential universes. 
  Computer does not create new information, it just traces out (discovers) one of the $2^n$ possible worlds that exists regardless of computer running or not.
  Relation to the Everettian Many-Worlds Interpretation. 


\item[\textbf{2022}] - From DocBook to LaTeX

  The manuscript was converted from DocBook to LaTeX, and the first version of this book was published on \href{https://meskanen.com/ghghnd/}{meskanen.com/ghghnd}.
